How it works

From a glance to a better shelf

Step 1

Map the shelf

A small camera on the shelf faces shoppers. We photograph the shelf straight on and mark where each brand sits, then calibrate the camera to it.

Step 2

Read attention

An in-store box estimates where each head is pointed and which brand that lands on. Live frames stay in memory and are never saved.

Step 3

Join with billing

Looks per brand are set against units sold for the same brand and hours, from your billing export. Every brand gets a sold-per-100-looks figure.

Step 4

Test and repeat

Change a price, a facing or a shelf level, and compare it with a shelf you left alone. Keep what earns more.

Why AisleLoop

Attention tools exist. Few tie attention to what sold

Shelf eye-tracking is sold today as a research tool, and CCTV analytics counts footfall. AisleLoop joins shelf attention to brand-level sales and lets brands fund the hardware. We have not found another product that combines the two.

Measurement

Brand-area attention, not product guesses

Head direction is projected onto a calibrated photo of the shelf and counted per brand area. That is reliable at brand level without eye-tracking hardware on every shelf.

Sales join

Attention joined to billing

Looks per brand meet units sold for the same hours. The result is sold per 100 looks, the shelf version of an online conversion rate.

Testing

Every change is a test

Recommendations run against an untouched comparison shelf, so stores and brands see measured effects instead of claims.

Business model

Brands fund the shelves

FMCG brands already pay for display space. Paying for proof that the display gets noticed is meant to cover the hardware cost with brand revenue, so stores could start for free.

Privacy

Edge-only by design

All vision runs on a box inside the store and only counts leave it. That eases store and shopper concerns, and keeps cloud video costs at zero.

Benchmark

A shared shelf benchmark

With store opt-in, anonymised results build a benchmark of what attention and conversion look like for each category and shelf position. It grows with every store.

Privacy by design

The video never leaves the store

AisleLoop counts attention. It does not identify shoppers and keeps no record of any individual.

Live video stays in memory

The in-store box reads each frame, works out head direction, and drops the frame. Live video is never recorded by AisleLoop.

No facial recognition in the standard product

Faces are used only to estimate where someone is looking. Setup photos are of the shelf, with any faces blurred.

Only counts leave the building

Looks, stare time and sales figures per brand, per hour. No images, no tracks, no profiles.

Shoppers are told

Signs at the entrance and on monitored shelves explain what is measured and why.
